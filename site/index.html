One team, people and agents.

Henosis is the shared, living session for a team's long-running AI agents, where agents are members of the team. A new engineer and a new agent enter the same circle on the same day: the same groups and chats, the same plan-first manners, the same release gate held by the people who consume the code, and the same manager's view.

The session

The session everyone can drop into.

Agents now run for hours, days and weeks, and work at that scale pulls in many people. Every agent product today gives the run to one person. Henosis makes the session the shared object: anyone on the team drops in, watches, steers, approves, forks, unites and hands off.

When two people steer at once, the kernel decides, in the open: authority, recency and scope compose their directives into one intent, and peers who disagree raise a contention the agent works around until someone with the baton resolves it. Who told the agent to do that is a lookup.

The council

A team's models, deliberating.

Every member brings a model of their own: a person's agent, a manager's agent, a team's shared agent. A council convenes several of them on one task so they deliberate the way a good team does. Each answers alone first; then they discuss as a group; then a chair writes the plan the team rates; then the work is split among the members' agents, who report back into the same room.

People sit in the room throughout. They brief, interject, instruct one model in private, rate and decide. A council gives a team several perspectives it chose itself, a record of who argued what, and a plan with named owners, every word and token in a replayable log.

Plan first, rate, then ship

Nothing is built before the team has rated the plan. Nothing ships before the people who will live with it have rated the release.

A session proposes its steps with token estimates before it may change anything. The team rates the plan one to five, the kernel approves it in the fold, and each step records what it actually cost. Token spend is planned, not discovered.

A push or a deploy is a release gate. It waits until enough raters approve it with a high enough average, and the raters are the people who consume the code, not only the person who prompted it. Every approval is bound to the hash of the exact call.

Editions

The same product from two people to a company of thousands.

One kernel, one product. What changes with the size of the organisation is where the state lives, who answers "who is this person", how many sessions one process holds, and what the auditors need to export. A session log written by a startup's single server folds to the same state in an enterprise's sharded cluster, and henosis verify proves it in both.
