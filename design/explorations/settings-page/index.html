Settings

One 760px column, six plain headings, rows beneath. The personal half (Profile, Notifications, Appearance, Keyboard) is what this person sees everywhere; the team half (Integrations, Team policy) sits under an apricot crease and says who may change it. Every control is native: text inputs with a hairline, radios and checkboxes carrying the one accent, no toggles, no save button. The only bordered box is a quiet notice for a policy that changed since this person last looked.

As Dee, the lead of Payments

Dee opens Settings from the sidebar. Her own rows come first. Because she leads Payments, the Team policy rows are live radios; Ana would see the same rows with the chosen word and the line "Dee sets these".

Team policy

Who approves a tool call, by risk class. Only humans vote; a deny by anyone eligible denies. Sessions in Payments inherit these unless a lead constrains one further.

Irreversible calls now need two drivers or owners

Read readReading files, logs and the ledger. Nothing leaves the sandbox.

Each change is an event in the project's log, attributed to you. Policy changed by Dee, Monday 09:14.

Rows, not panelsEvery setting is a label with a one-line reason on the left and its control on the right; a section is a heading over rows. No tabs, no cards.
Words for rulesThe approval rule for a risk class is a sentence fragment chosen by radio: None, One contributor, The driver, One owner, Two drivers or owners. The default is marked, not pre-styled.
Team under a creaseThe apricot hairline that separates chrome from content also separates what is yours from what is the team's, with one line saying who may change it.
