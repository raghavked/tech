The app inside the window

Fold's desktop shell is Tauri around the same web client. The question is where the operating system's chrome stops and ours begins. One answer for both platforms: the window has no titlebar of its own; the sidebar runs up into the title region and holds the system's controls, and the main column's title row is the drag region. The native titlebar alternative is shown below it, then the states that make or break a custom titlebar.

macOS, custom titlebar

Traffic lights inset in the sidebar's top row; the wordmark beside them; the session title in the main column's first row. The whole top band is draggable.

The apricot wash marks the drag region: the whole title row on both platforms, minus any button in it. In macOS fullscreen the traffic lights leave and the row shrinks from 52 to 40px, so the wordmark slides to the left edge; nothing else moves. When the sidebar collapses on macOS the lights stay where they are and the main column's row starts after them, with the sidebar toggle and the project crumb. On Windows the maximised window loses its radius and the maximise glyph becomes restore; inactive windows dim the title and the controls, never the content.

E

One rule set, two platforms

Title rowmacOS 52px (40px in fullscreen); Windows 40px. The sidebar's row and the main column's row are the same height, so the crease between them is one unbroken line from the top edge.
System controlsmacOS: traffic lights at (14, 20) inside the sidebar, drawn by the system. Windows: minimise, maximise, close at 46×40 at the top right, drawn by the app, hit-tested as the system's.
App identityThe mark and wordmark sit at the top-left of the sidebar, after the lights on macOS, at the edge on Windows. Never in the window title: the OS title is the session's name, for Mission Control and Alt-Tab.
Drag regionThe whole title row across sidebar and main column. Double-click zooms on macOS and maximises on Windows; the sidebar toggle, the crumb and the right-hand buttons are excluded.
Collapsed sidebarmacOS keeps the lights and starts the main row at 84px; Windows starts it at the edge. The toggle and the project crumb take the sidebar's place in the row.
InactiveTitle and controls go to the tertiary grey; the sidebar, stream and composer do not change. The crease stays apricot.
WebThe same shell with a 48px row and no controls; the browser owns the rest. Nothing in the layout is desktop-only except the inset for the controls.
