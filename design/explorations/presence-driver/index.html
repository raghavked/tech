Presence and the driver

One agent, many humans. Presence is the quiet answer to "who else is in here, and who has the wheel". It is never logged, so it never gets chrome: an avatar, a ring, a sentence. The driver is the one human who holds the seat; the apricot ring says so, and handing off is the ring moving. Everything below is built from the 24 px avatar, the 16 px avatar, five verbs and one button.

1Two sizes, four states

24 px is the working avatar: the top bar, people rows, the stack by the title. 16 px is the inline avatar: a presence line, a sidebar agent card, a step in the log. The ring sits outside the avatar's own paper border, so it is always a ring and never a thicker edge. "Away" is opacity, not grey; "offered" is a dashed ring that becomes solid on acceptance.

2The stack

The driver is always first and always ringed, so the eye finds the seat before it counts heads. Then whoever is present, in order of joining; away people fall off the stack and into the count. Past four, the rest is a number, not a fifth face. The stack is one control: a button that opens "who is here".

3Five verbs

Presence is a sentence in the voice of the brand: a name in the serif, a verb in the sans. The verb carries the state; colour does not. Only "driving" gets the ring, and only "writing" gets motion, three dots that pulse while a directive or a vote is being typed. Observers are a count with a thin eye, not a row of faces.

4In the top bar

The 48 px bar keeps the title on the left and presence on the right: the stack, one sentence, the count. The sentence changes with the state, so the bar reads "Ana is driving" most of the day, "Bo is writing a directive" for a few seconds, and "Dee is offered the seat" during a handoff. 5Who is here

Pressing the stack opens one quiet list under it. Rows, not cards: a name, a verb, a lead's title where it matters. The driver's own row carries the ring and the only coloured dot; present people are green, away people fade. The one action lives at the foot, and only the driver sees it.

Order. Driver, then present people by joining, then away. The list never re-sorts while open.

Verbs. driving · writing a directive · writing a vote · reading turn n · away n. Six words that any teammate, and Slack, can say.

What is not here. No roles unless they explain authority (lead, owner). No "last seen" timestamps. No status pills; the dot and the word are enough.

Hand off. Choosing it opens the offer notice in the stream, as in the handoff exploration. Non-drivers see "Ask to drive" instead, which posts a one-line request to the driver.

6The handoff affordance, from three chairs

The same bar, three people. Only the driver can give the seat away; a contributor can ask; an observer can only watch and read the brief. The affordance is one small button whose label changes with the chair. It is never primary: the accent stays on the composer's send arrow.

7Above the composer

"Bo is writing a directive" matters most where it changes what you are about to do: just above your own composer. One line in the gutter, 16 px avatar, no box, gone ten seconds after Bo stops. The observer count sits at the far end of the same line so a driver typing something risky knows who is watching.

All of it at once, in the desktop shell at 1000 px: the 16 px stacks on the agents rail, the 24 px stack and sentence in the bar, the ring on Ana, the seat divider from this morning's handoff, and Bo's line above the composer.
