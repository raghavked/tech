Command palette

One field, opened with ⌘K from anywhere in the desktop or web app. Empty, it lists what you can do in the session you are looking at, then where you were. Typed, it answers from three places in one list: an action, the sessions in the project, and what the team already knows. Nothing in it is a card; every line is a row with a verb or a name on the left and its context in grey on the right.

Dee presses ⌘K in Ana's session. The first group is the verbs of this session, ordered by what is waiting; the approval she owes is the second row and says so in words. Below it, the sessions she was in last.

Grammar

The palette at rest, out of its frame, with the rules it keeps.

This session
Team memory
↑↓ move↵ runesc close

One list, groups as words. "This session", "Go to", "Recent"; typed, "Action", "Sessions", "Team memory" with a count. No tabs, no filters, no sidebar inside the palette.

A row is icon, words, context. A 16px line icon, the verb or the name, then grey context on the right: who it goes to, what state it is in, who said it and in which commit. Status is a word with a dot, never a pill.

Verb first, consequence in words. "Approve delete legacy_invoices · needs you · 1 of 2 drivers · cannot be undone". The irreversible one has no red; it has a sentence.

Selection is the apricot bar, the same two pixels the sidebar uses for the open session. Matches are underlined in apricot, not highlighted. The palette carries no accent of its own; while it is open the send arrow beneath it is ink.

Any text is a steer. Words that match no command are offered back as a directive to the agent in view, so ⌘K is also the fastest way to talk to it.
