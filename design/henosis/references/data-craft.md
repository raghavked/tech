# Data craft: what the best work does, and ten elements for Henosis

## What the best work does

**Word-sized graphics, not chart occasions.** Tufte's definition sets the bar: a sparkline is "a small, intense, simple, word-sized graphic with typographic resolution", with a data-ink ratio of 1.0, no frame, no ticks, scaled by the words and numbers beside it. His glucose example ties the last point to the number beside it with one colour accent and lays a grey band for the normal range, so deviation reads without an axis. Aspect ratio follows Cleveland's 45-degree rule: lumpy, not spiky, not flat ([Sparkline theory and practice](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/); [Sparklines history, 1324 to now](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparklines-history-by-tufte-1324-to-now/)).

**One hue, and grey does the rest.** Datawrapper's rule: grey everything first, then colour only what the sentence is about; a desaturated step of the same hue is the only alternative to grey. Greys are warmed to match the palette (The Economist's `RGB(207,209,195)`), and a highlight's label is drawn a shade darker than the mark so thin letters stay legible ([Emphasize with color](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/emphasize-with-color-in-data-visualizations); [Colors for style guides](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/colors-for-data-vis-style-guides); [Remind readers of the colors](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/remind-readers-of-colors-in-data-vis)). Stripe's home screen is the product version: five numbers, monochrome sparklines, previous period in smaller text, colour reserved for status, a 2 px line with no markers, gridlines near 10 % opacity ([Stripe dashboard breakdown](https://www.925studios.co/blog/stripe-dashboard-design-breakdown); [Stripe chart layout](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/chart-layout)). Observable Plot's defaults agree: grid at 0.1 opacity, axes as text marks, facets as the route to small multiples ([Grid mark](https://observablehq.com/plot/marks/grid); [Facets](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/facets); [Crafting data colors](https://observablehq.com/blog/crafting-data-colors)).

**Small multiples with shared context.** Muth's small-multiple guide: go small, keep only panels that carry the point, sort by end value or change, repeat every line in grey behind each panel so comparison survives the split, and let colour categorise panels since names no longer need hues ([Small multiple line charts](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/what-to-consider-when-creating-small-multiple-line-charts)).

**Annotation is the chart.** Two type levels only (12 px grey, 14 px ink), left-aligned, a canvas-coloured outline where text crosses a line; note, connector and subject are separable, and the connector goes when the note sits beside its subject in the same colour ([Text in data visualizations](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/text-in-data-visualizations); [Annotation anatomy](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/text-annotations-anatomy)). Nadieh Bremer builds whole pieces this way: a day of births wrapped into a ring, warm above average and cool below, concentric clipped circles instead of a smooth gradient, hour labels on `textPath`, annotations for the three moments worth naming ([WebExpo live-coding](https://webexpo.net/blog/from-a-blank-screen-to-birth-rhythms-nadieh-bremer-live-codes-a-radial-story/); [CHART sample chapters](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/img/books/chart/CHART_SamplePages.pdf)).

**Honest trend, honest density.** Vercel replaced Analytics' point-to-point line with a fitted curve because the first-to-last delta "felt wrong" ([Curve fitting for charts](https://vercel.com/blog/curve-fitting-for-charts-better-visualizations-for-vercel-analytics)). Linear keeps analytics in the sidebar of the view you are in; a bar's hover shows percentiles, a selection filters the list, and cycle graphs are grey scope, solid completed, dotted target ([Linear Insights docs](https://linear.app/docs/insights); [Insights and analytics](https://linear.app/learn/insights-and-analytics)). Bloomberg's lesson: density is the product, colour-vision work is measured in a lab, and restraint on a dense screen means one pattern carried across every workflow ([Concealing complexity](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/how-bloomberg-terminal-ux-designers-conceal-complexity/); [Color accessibility](https://www.bloomberg.com/ux/2021/10/14/designing-the-terminal-for-color-accessibility/)). Token dashboards in the wild stack bars by model and gauge budgets green-amber-red; the better ones split every measure between people and agents and let a bar's drill-down name who spent ([Guild usage dashboard](https://docs.guild.ai/insights/usage); [MintMCP usage](https://www.mintmcp.com/docs/agent-monitor-usage)).

## Ten elements for Henosis

Palette rule for all ten: data ink is Dress Blues `#2A3244` stepped in lightness only, spend is Chocolate `#56352D`, the living thing is Apricot `#E2C4A6`, context is warm grey `--surface-3`; status hues appear only against a budget or a gate.

1. **Spark-words.** Inline `<svg width="4.5em" height="1em">` with `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, a `currentColor` path, and a 2 px `fill="var(--apricot)"` circle on the last point. *Session stream, Project page.* A sentence like "Ana's session spent 3.6k ▁▂▃▅▆ today" puts a person and her agent on one line of prose.

2. **Normal-range band.** A `<rect>` at `fill: var(--surface-3)` spanning the team's median-to-p75 behind every sparkline; the line only colours outside it. *Usage tiles, Manager overview.* Everyone's work is read against the team's own normal, not against zero.

3. **Shared-shadow multiples.** Per-person panels on one `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(140px, 1fr))`; every other line repeated in each panel at `stroke-opacity: .18`, sorted by end value. *Usage by person and by agent.* Agents and people share the panel grid, so an agent is just another panel in the same sort.

4. **Hairline tally bars.** Tokens by day as `<rect>` capped at 24 px with `rx` on the output segment only; input is a lightness step of Dress Blues, output Chocolate, cache a dotted outline beside the bar. *Usage, Plan card totals.* Who held the session sits in the avatar at the foot; steering changes no colour.

5. **Pace line with its band.** Cumulative spend as a `<path>` solid so far, `stroke-dasharray: 4 3` at this pace, the ±8 % band as a `<path fill-opacity=".12">`, the 80 % tick as a 1 px amber `<line>` with one labelled dot where the pace crosses. *Usage, Cost forecast.* The forecast is one hand drawn for the whole team; the amber dot is dated, never a percentage.

6. **Rating glyph.** Five 6 px `<circle>` elements; fill `var(--accent)` for filled stars, `stroke: var(--line)` for empty, and a 1 px `<line>` under the row at the required average. *Plan card, Approvals queue.* Two raters' glyphs stack in a `flex` column over the same baseline, so "needs 2 at 3+" is a picture before it is a sentence.

7. **Estimate tick on a meter.** A `height: 3px` track with `background: linear-gradient(90deg, var(--accent) var(--pct), var(--surface-3) 0)` and a `::after` 1 px tick at `left: var(--est)` labelled "est" in mono. *Plan card, Token meter everywhere.* Under or over estimate reads as a gap between fill and tick in one hue; the agent's plan and the team's budget share a track.

8. **Direct labels, no legend.** `<text>` at the end of each line, `paint-order: stroke; stroke: var(--canvas); stroke-width: 3px`, label a shade darker than its mark. *Every chart.* Names of people and agents sit at the tips of their own lines in the same serif, one type level for both.

9. **Dense-first row.** Stat tiles at `gap: 8px`, 11 px mono labels, value in Fraunces at 28 px, delta in ink with its sign and no colour unless against a budget; filters live in a quieter 24 px-padded strip. *Manager overview, Usage.* The number is one team's number; the breakdown is the only place a person or agent is named.

10. **Session ring.** The brand's two arcs reused as data: a `<circle>` with `pathLength="100"`, `stroke-dasharray: var(--people) var(--gap)` for the chocolate arc and a second circle offset for the apricot agent arc, closing to a ring when the plan is done. *Project page header, Mobile session.* One ring, two arcs, people and agents each drawing their share of the same whole.
