# Data craft: what the best work does, and ten elements for Henosis

Henosis is one session where a team's people and agents work together. Its numbers should read like the rest of the product: typographic, warm, one hue at a time. Sources read October 2026.

## What the best work does

**Graphics at the resolution of type.** Tufte's sparkline is "a small, intense, simple, word-sized graphic with typographic resolution": the last point and the number beside it share one colour accent, the normal range is a grey band so deviation reads without an axis, and 14 letterspaces of sparkline carry 14,000 readings. Tables of current values reinforce recency bias; "sparklines improve the attention span of tables" ([Sparkline theory and practice](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/)).

**Small multiples, sorted, with the rest in grey.** Datawrapper's guide: go small, keep only the panels that carry the point, sort by end value or change and say so, repeat every line faintly behind each panel so comparison survives, and since panel titles replace a legend, colour is freed to categorise or highlight ([What to consider](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/what-to-consider-when-creating-small-multiple-line-charts); [Multiple lines launch](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/small-multiple-line-charts)). Its sibling is the highlighted line: one series in the accent, on top, with a halo; everything else grey ([data.europa guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/number-of-series)). Observable Plot makes faceting a channel (`fx`/`fy`) and labels a facet with a text mark instead of an axis ([Plot facets](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/facets)).

**Fewer hues, more shades.** Muth's palette guide names the approach for brand-led products: "fewer hues, more shades", one grey for data and another for everything that is not, gradients built from one hue ([Colors for style guides](https://blog.datawrapper.de/colors-for-data-vis-style-guides/)). Bloomberg is the extreme case: amber is the non-semantic text colour and protected brand property; red and green carry only direction, and the colour-vision schemes swap that pair to blue/red while leaving amber alone ([Color accessibility](https://www.bloomberg.com/ux/2021/10/14/designing-the-terminal-for-color-accessibility/); [Design ethos](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/bloombergs-customer-centric-design-ethos/)). Ström on the Terminal: density works when every mark is data-ink ([UI density](https://matthewstrom.com/writing/ui-density)).

**Annotation instead of apparatus.** Datawrapper omits y-axis labels on purpose: the title says what is measured, the unit is appended to the first tick, the rest is annotation beside the data ([Why no axis labels](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/why-datawrapper-does-not-include-axis-labels-for-many-charts)). Annotations get a text outline, are sized in chart width not pixels, and on narrow screens become numbered notes below the chart ([Responsive annotations](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/better-more-responsive-annotations-in-Datawrapper-data-visualizations/)). Plot's `tip` mark is the same in code: a static note at an x,y with `anchor: "bottom"` and a small `dy` ([Tip mark](https://observablehq.com/plot/marks/tip)).

**Material from SVG, not decoration.** Nadieh Bremer shows how little it takes: a smooth legend is one `linearGradient` with two stops; a glow is `feGaussianBlur` merged under `SourceGraphic`; a per-datum gradient is a `<radialGradient>` per element with data-driven stops, the bright spot turned toward what lights it ([Smooth legend](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/05/smooth-color-legend-d3-svg-gradient/); [Glow](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/06/glow-filter-d3-visualization/); [Data-based gradients](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/05/data-based-svg-gradient-d3/)).

**Analytics inside the view, not beside it.** Linear's Insights live in the sidebar of the list you are looking at; hovering a bar gives percentiles, selecting it filters the list, and the cycle graph is grey scope, solid completed, dotted target ([Insights docs](https://linear.app/docs/insights); [Insights and analytics](https://linear.app/learn/insights-and-analytics)). Vercel's AI Gateway sets the spend baseline: input and output tokens, spend by model and period, budgets per team, project or key with a hard stop ([Observability and spend](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/observability-and-spend)). None show a person's spend beside an agent's; that is the gap Henosis fills.

## Ten elements for Henosis

Palette rule for all ten: data ink is Dress Blues `#2A3244` stepped in lightness only; spend is Chocolate Fondant `#56352D`; the current, living thing is Apricot Illusion `#E2C4A6`; context is warm grey off the cream canvas. Status hues appear only against a budget or a gate. Numbers in Inter `tabular-nums`; headline values in Fraunces.

1. **Spark-word.** Inline `<svg width="5em" height="1em" viewBox="0 0 100 20" preserveAspectRatio="none">` with a `stroke="currentColor"` path, `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, and a `fill="#E2C4A6"` dot on the last point tied to the number that follows. *Session stream, member row.* "Ana and her two agents spent 3.6k today" is one sentence, one graphic.

2. **Team-normal band.** A `<rect fill="var(--surface-3)">` from team p25 to p75 behind every sparkline; the stroke turns Chocolate only outside it. *Usage tiles, Manager overview.* People and agents are read against the same quiet band, not against zero.

3. **Shadow multiples.** `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(140px, 1fr))`, one panel per participant, every other series repeated at `stroke-opacity: .16`, sorted by end value with the sort named in the caption. *Usage by participant.* An agent is a panel in the same sort as a person, nothing more.

4. **Hairline tally.** Daily tokens as a `<rect>` stack, input in a lightness step of Dress Blues, output in Chocolate, cached input as a `stroke-dasharray="1 2"` outline; height capped at 24px, `rx="1"` on the top segment. *Usage, Plan card footer.* The avatar under each bar is whoever held the session; steering changes nothing.

5. **Pace line.** Cumulative spend as a solid `<path>`, the projection as `stroke-dasharray: 4 3`, a `fill-opacity=".12"` band for the pace range, and the budget as a 1px `<line>` with a single labelled dot where projection meets it. *Usage, Cost forecast.* One hand drawn for the whole team; the dot carries a date, never a percentage.

6. **Rating dots.** Five 6px `<circle>` marks, Apricot fill for earned, `stroke: var(--line)` for empty, a `<line>` under the row at the required average; raters' rows stack in a `flex` column on one baseline. *Plan card, Approvals.* "Needs two at 3 or above" is a picture before it is a sentence; a person's row and an agent's row are the same row.

7. **Estimate tick.** A 3px track, `background: linear-gradient(90deg, var(--spend) var(--pct), var(--surface-3) 0)`, and a `::after` 1px tick at `left: var(--est)` labelled "est" in 11px mono. *Plan card, every token meter.* Over or under estimate is a gap in one hue; the agent's plan and the team's budget share a track.

8. **Outlined direct labels.** `<text>` at each line's end with `paint-order: stroke; stroke: var(--canvas); stroke-width: 3px`, set one shade darker than its mark; no legend anywhere. *Every chart.* Names of people and agents sit at the tips of their own lines in the same face and size.

9. **Dense stat row.** Tiles at `gap: 8px`, 11px mono labels, the value in Fraunces 28px with `font-optical-sizing: auto`, the delta in ink with its sign, coloured only when it crosses a budget; filters in a quieter strip below. *Manager overview.* The headline is the team's number; only the breakdown names anyone.

10. **Session ring.** The brand mark as data: two `<circle pathLength="100">` elements sharing a centre, `stroke-dasharray: var(--people) 100` in Chocolate and `stroke-dasharray: var(--agents) 100` with `stroke-dashoffset` in Apricot, closing into a ring when the plan is done; an `feGaussianBlur` glow only at the join. *Project header, mobile session.* Two arcs, two kinds of participant, one whole.
