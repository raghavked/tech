# Editorial type in software: what the best work does, and ten elements for Henosis

Research pass, October 2026. The brief's faces are Fraunces (display serif) and Inter (UI sans); `tokens.css` still ships Instrument Serif and Instrument Sans, and every element below ports by swapping the family name.

## What the best work does

**It lets the optical-size axis do the work.** Fraunces carries wght, opsz, SOFT and WONK. Below 18px the opsz axis opens letterspacing, raises the x-height, drops contrast and swaps the leaning h/n/m out automatically; browsers apply it from `font-size` when `font-optical-sizing: auto` is on, and `font-variation-settings: "opsz" 8` overrides it on purpose ([Fraunces minisite](https://fraunces.undercase.xyz/); [Google Design](https://design.google/library/a-new-take-on-old-style-typeface); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-optical-sizing)). Inter 4 answers from the sans side: six Display designs on an opsz axis from 14 to 32, tall x-height and ink traps for text, cleaner curves for display, plus `calt` that reshapes punctuation and arrows by context ([rsms.me/inter](https://rsms.me/inter/); [Inter 4.0 release](https://github.com/rsms/inter/releases/tag/v4.0)).

**The pairing is serif for the gesture, sans for the work.** Instrument built a narrow display serif and a workhorse sans as one family, "harmony through a shared anatomy, or contrast through dramatic sizing"; the serif is meant for large sizes only ([Instrument rebrand](https://www.instrument.com/latest/from-old-to-bold-instrument-rebrand-signals-energetic-shift); [instrument-serif](https://github.com/instrument/instrument-serif)). The Browser Company's Dia sets a 96px weight-300 sans headline and interrupts it with one serif word at weight 650, "quiet copy with a single loud gesture", and keeps the serif out of body text entirely ([Tastekit, Dia](https://tastekit.dev/apps/dia/marketing/DESIGN.md)). Figma's Config 2024 site used Aperçu Mono as "a small, structural monospace" against expressive supergraphics; Config 2025 let a condensed cut "sing as display" while regular Figma Sans carried the dense agenda ([Config 2024](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-2024-branding/); [Config 2025](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-we-shaped-the-visual-identity-for-config-2025/)).

**Restraint is the signature.** Stripe Press sets everything, buttons included, in the Ivar superfamily: 25px reading text at 1.5, labels at 600 with +0.32px tracking, square corners, and a graphic vocabulary of registration marks and framing ticks borrowed from print production ([This Old Machine](https://thisoldmachine.com/stripe-press); [Tastekit, Stripe Press](https://tastekit.dev/gallery/apps/stripe-press)). Are.na commissioned Areal, a redrawn Arial, so the interface would "fade into the background"; headings and body share one size, hierarchy comes from weight, and Dinamo added a dark-mode axis ([Are.na Editorial](https://www.are.na/editorial/introducing-areal-are-nas-new-typeface); [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/newsletter/the-dinamo-update-areal-custom-typeface-arena)).

**The mono is a sibling, not a guest.** Klim drew Söhne from the Halbfett outward and added Söhne Mono last, narrow and tightly spaced to match, with tabular lining figures and slashed zero ([Söhne design information](https://klim.co.nz/blog/soehne-design-information/)). Vercel went the other way: Geist Mono first, then Sans, both with a high x-height and short descenders so they sit together ([vercel.com/font](https://vercel.com/font); [basement.studio](https://basement.studio/post/the-birth-of-geist-a-typeface-crafted-for-the-web)).

**Numerals and measure are decisions.** Lining figures for headings and anything scanned; oldstyle for running text; tabular lining for tables and for "numbers that change but need to stay aligned, such as a countdown" ([Rutter, Numerals and tables](https://book.webtypography.net/Web-Typography_Numerals-and-tables.pdf); [Google Fonts Knowledge](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/introducing_type/understanding_numerals); [MDN font-variant-numeric](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-variant-numeric)). Measure 45 to 75 characters, leading around 1.5, and one line-height as the magic number every margin multiplies ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2011/03/technical-web-typography-guidelines-and-techniques/)).

## Ten elements for Henosis

1. **The accent word.** `.accent{font:italic 400 1.1em Fraunces; font-variation-settings:"opsz" 20,"SOFT" 100; color:#56352D}` inline in an Inter sentence. Screen: session dividers ("Cy joins the circle"), the composer hint ("to the team"), the Goal label. The serif names a relationship, never a speaker; a person's joining and an agent's handoff get the same italic.

2. **x-height lock.** `.who{font-family:Fraunces; font-size-adjust:0.53}` so the serif name matches Inter's x-height beside it (Inter is 1118/2048 at opsz 14). Screen: message meta, toasts (serif who, sans sentence). "Ana" and "Billing page" read at one apparent size on one baseline.

3. **Serif tuned by ground, not by author.** `:root[data-theme=dark] .serif{font-variation-settings:"opsz" 9,"SOFT" 100}` lowers contrast on navy the way Areal's dark axis does; light keeps `auto`. Screen: session-dark. The same cut serves everyone in the room, and only the canvas changes it.

4. **Stable counters.** `.meta{font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums lining-nums; min-inline-size:14ch; text-align:end}`. Screen: the right-hand meta column, the replay label ("Turn 8 · 09:44 · Bo steers"), the token chip. A person's "09:44" and the agent's "turn 7 · step 3 of 4" share one figure width, so nothing jiggles as either advances.

5. **Figures by register.** `.serif{font-variant-numeric:oldstyle-nums}` in recap and divider prose ("6 more turns to now"); `h1,h2,.chip{font-variant-numeric:lining-nums}`. Screen: project page recap, title row, pills. Numbers in sentences belong to the sentence whoever produced it; numbers in chrome stand up straight.

6. **One rag for all prose.** `.msg .text{max-inline-size:66ch; text-wrap:pretty; hyphens:auto; line-height:1.5}` on human bubbles and agent prose alike. Screen: session stream. A four-minute human reply and a four-hundred-millisecond agent reply wrap by the same rule.

7. **Contextual punctuation in chips.** `.chip,.step{font-feature-settings:"calt","case","tnum"}` so Inter centres brackets and hyphens on caps and digits and turns `->` into an arrow in "Then: write …" and "Ana -> Bo". Screen: composer scope chip, step grid, the baton line. The arrow between two people and the arrow between a person and an agent are one glyph.

8. **Registration ticks.** Four corner marks via `mask: conic-gradient(...)` or an inline `<svg><path d="M0 8V0h8"/></svg>` at each corner, 1px Dress Blues, Stripe Press's framing device. Screen: release gate, contention direction cards, handoff brief. Whatever is inside the ticks is being made; the frame does not care whose direction it holds.

9. **The one heavy word.** `.recap h2{font:300 2.5rem/1.05 "Inter Display"; letter-spacing:-0.03em} .recap h2 b{font:600 1em Fraunces; font-variation-settings:"opsz" 40,"WONK" 1}`. Screen: project page header, manager overview ("Billing page **shipped**"). The heavy word is the outcome, and the outcome belongs to the room.

10. **Hung quotes.** `blockquote{hanging-punctuation:first last; text-indent:-0.4ch}` with `@supports not (hanging-punctuation:first)` keeping the indent. Screen: the handoff offerer's quote, rating notes, the agent's "What I see:" note. People's words and the agent's words are quoted with the same editorial care, marks in the margin, text on the measure.
