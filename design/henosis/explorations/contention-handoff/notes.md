# Contention and the baton · notes

## The idea
A contention is two directions laid side by side, not a warning: the notice names the scope in one serif sentence ("Two directions for `proration`."), says in plain words who picks and when you may ("until Ana picks one · you own this session, so you can too · policy block"), shows each direction as a card with its author, time, scope, a Pick button and the agent's one-line read of the consequence ("matches the PDF" / "moves 3 fixtures by a cent"), and lets the agent say what it sees underneath while it keeps working around the held scope. A handoff is a baton that visibly moves between two avatars: the offer notice carries the offerer's words as a quote and a three-column brief computed from the ledger (situation, open items, since you were here), Accept makes you the driver and Ana stays as a contributor, and both notices close the same way, green for a moment, then folded into a divider in the product's voice ("Bo picked Cy's direction for proration", "Bo has the baton"). The seat decides what you can press: the driver sees her own direction ringed with Withdraw, an owner can pick too, a contributor gets the sentence and a way to speak, and the offerer sees the baton nudging toward the other person with a way to take the offer back.

## What to keep
- The stripe colour as the kind: amber for a contention (held), apricot for the baton (something live moving between people), green for the moment either closes, then a divider.
- The shared head: a serif sentence, a plain rule naming who can act, elapsed time on the right with the pulsing dot.
- Direction cards side by side with a number (1, 2) that is also the keyboard shortcut; "2 · yours" ringed in apricot with Withdraw; "picked" in green and the loser struck through in the resolved state.
- The agent's note ("What I see: …") inside the contention: it does not pick, it tells the picker what each direction costs.
- The held step in the agent's message ("proration · held for a pick") and the rail card ("Holds proration for a pick · rendering the tax lines"), so the hold is visible outside the notice.
- The baton pass: two avatars, a dashed lane, the apricot stick with a chocolate centre, nudging while waiting, landed with a ripple on accept; the driver ring moves with it in the topbar and the drawer.
- The computed brief as three cards in the offer, with the hot item first; the full brief lands in Details under Intent after accept.
- Drawer Team tab: "Here now" with the offered row in apricot wash and a 40 s pill, "The baton" as a history, open and resolved contentions, and the contention policy as radios with one line each.
- Mobile: rail and drawer fold away, the two directions stack, the brief stacks, the baton pass sits above the sentence, keyboard legends disappear.

## Open questions
- Should an owner's pick be allowed while the driver is online and has seen the contention, or only after a grace period, to keep "the driver resolves it" true in practice?
- The agent's "What I see" line: is it always worth a model call, or only when a fixture or test can be run cheaply to give a real consequence?
- Under `latest-wins` and `driver-wins` the contention resolves itself; should the notice still appear for a moment (as a divider only) so the loser learns their steer was overridden?
- Does the offer expire (and the baton return to the offerer) if the recipient is away, and should the toast on the phone show the brief or only the sentence?
- A third steer on a held scope: does it join the contention as a third card (as drawn in the contributor's hint) or replace the author's earlier one?
