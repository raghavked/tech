# Illustration · notes

## The idea
Henosis needs no characters, hands or isometric desks: the mark already says the whole product, so every spot illustration is the ring in a different moment. Twelve spots (Open, Joining, Whole, Crew, Baton, Gate, Plan, Unite, Memory, Inbox, Contention, Meter) are built from one grammar: a 96-unit box, the two arcs at radius 28 and stroke 10 with round caps, four inks (chocolate for the person's arc, navy for the agent's, apricot for what is live, a dashed ghost for what has not happened), and the only moves allowed are translating, rotating, dashing or shortening the arcs plus smaller rings and concentric ripples. Each spot carries its own sentence in the product's voice ("No sessions on Billing page yet", "Bo has the baton", "The gate waits for two ratings") and knows which page it belongs to, so an empty state is the spot at 96, a card gets it at 48, a row at 24, and marketing composes many rings converging on one.

## What to keep
- One grammar, no new shapes. A spot is only ever the arcs moved, rotated, dashed or shortened, plus rings of the same family and ripples concentric to the centre. The twelve are drawn once as SVG symbols and reused everywhere; adding a thirteenth means naming a product moment first.
- The dot is a promise: solid apricot means something is here, dashed means it is expected, absent means the spot is not about presence (Meter). Amber appears exactly once, in Contention.
- The colour mapping per ground: chocolate and navy on cream (as the brief says for the mark on cream); apricot-deep and cream on navy and on the brand gradient; on the dark canvas chocolate lifts to #B27A66 and navy becomes cream, so the ring stays a ring without a disc behind it.
- Row size is its own drawing: stroke 14, radius 26, satellites dropped. Below 48 a spot falls back to Open, Whole or the dashed ring.
- Only one motion: Open becomes Whole with the brand's arcs-close keyframes when the first real thing lands. No bead runs, no ripple grows; every spot is designed to be read still.
- The don'ts: no team accent inside a spot, never one continuous circle (that is the older ring-and-bead), never flat caps, never a disc or blob behind a spot on cream.
- The product's empty-state sentence stays the page's sentence; the spot never gets a caption of its own.

## Open questions
- The dark-canvas chocolate (#B27A66) is a fifth brand colour in all but name. Is that acceptable, or should dark-mode spots go to apricot-deep and cream like the navy ground, accepting that the person's arc and the dot then share a hue?
- Crew draws two rings linked with one shared dot; Unite draws a branch leaving and returning. Are these too close to each other at 24px, and should Unite fall back to Whole in rows?
- Contention's amber bead sits where the two arcs overlap; the app's contention notice today is a text row. Does the notice earn a 48 spot, or is amber in an illustration one colour too many?
- Marketing: the hero composition shows eight satellites on an orbit. Is that the one composition for the whole site, or do landing, pricing and docs each get their own arrangement of the same rings?
- Meter at zero vs. Meter at 72%: the usage page could use the spot as a real data mark (the arc length is the spend). Should an illustration ever carry live numbers, or does it stay a picture and the budget bar stays the data?
- The screenshots: the light shot is the whole board at half scale and the dark shot is a 1440×900 crop of the twelve spots at full scale, to stay under the size budget. Is that the right pair for reviewers?
