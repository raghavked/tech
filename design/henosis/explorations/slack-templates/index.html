Slack templates

Henosis in Slack is the same team at a third distance: one sentence first, in the product's voice, with the buttons the app has. A session is a thread in its project channel. Approvals and contentions are buttons that vote and resolve without opening the app. Mentions in a team channel reach the agent, which answers in the thread. Leads get a brief every morning. The link into Henosis is always last.

Slack cannot do serif, so names are bold and session titles are italic: the two things the app sets in Instrument Serif. Tool names and paths stay in backticks. One emoji at most, at the front, and it is the kind: :henosis: for joining and the baton, :raised_hand: for an approval, :warning: for a contention, :white_check_mark: / :x: for the decision, :sunrise: for the brief.

Five parts, always in this order

1
The emblem. The mark on navy in Slack's 36 px square. A team with its own emblem (Team look) posts with that emblem on the brand gradient.
2
Henosis · APP · time. Never "bot", never "AI-powered". The app name is the product; the person is named in the sentence.
3
The sentence. The same words as the toast and the inbox row. The text fallback carries it whole, so push notifications read correctly.
4
The context line. Project, crew, driver, and the one number that matters (tokens, ratings, commits ahead). Slack's context block, 12.5 px, grey.
5
The buttons, then the link. At most five buttons; one primary (the recommended answer), one danger at the far side. "Open in Henosis" is a link, not a button, and is always last.

After a decision

  • The message edits itself: buttons go, the result line takes their place, and an (edited) mark shows it.
  • A thread reply says who answered and how ("Bo rated 4"), so the thread reads as the stream does.
  • Lines about one session within 1.5 s are folded into one reply, as the adapter does today.
Voice Calm, specific, warm. "Ana joins the circle." "The plan waits for two ratings." The sentence names the person, the session and what it means for the reader.
Where Project channel for sessions; team channel for mentions; management channel for contentions, memory conflicts and the brief. A contention also lands in both owners' threads.
Buttons Slack has three styles only: default, green primary, red danger. Primary marks the recommended answer. Rating buttons are five defaults with the 4 as primary under a ratings rule.
Roles A click is a vote by the Slack user's linked member. A contributor's rating counts; an observer's click answers "you can watch; ask for a brief". Unlinked users are asked to join first.

The root message is the session's address in Slack. Replies are directives: plain text steers main, [scope] aims at a branch, /constrain, /pause, /resume, /cancel do what they say. The app echoes each directive as a line so the thread reads like the stream, and folds progress lines that land within 1.5 s into one reply.

Under a ratings rule the five buttons are the vote: a tap sends vote(approve, rating) with that number and no note. The 4 is Slack-green because it is the default the app sends for a bare Approve, so the quickest tap and the app agree. Without a ratings rule the message shows Approve and Deny, nothing more. A note goes in the thread reply ("4 · reads right"), which the adapter parses as a rating with a note.

Channel
the session's thread; a copy to the owner's DM when the thread is muted
Who

actions · action_id

1★ … 5★vote:payments:s_7f2:ap_19:approve:n Approvevote:payments:s_7f2:ap_19:approve (sends 4) Denyvote:payments:s_7f2:ap_19:deny
  • Buttons carry value = the rating; the handler maps it to the app's vote call with rating.
  • Slack allows five elements per actions block comfortably; the Deny and the link go in a second actions block on narrow clients.
  • One rating per person: a second tap replaces the first and the thread says "Bo changed to 5".

Result states

  • granted · names each rater and rating, the average and the bar.
  • denied · names who and quotes the note; "The agent waits for a steer."
  • expired · "Nobody answered in 2 h; the agent paused." with Resume.
  • superseded · the plan was revised; buttons go, the line says which plan replaced it.

A contention is one event seen from three seats. Leads see both sides with the numbers that decide (who was first, how far along, what it cost) and the three resolutions; each owner sees their own write refused in their own thread, with what they can do now; nobody is @mentioned, the context line says whose turn it is. Resolution is one line in all three places.

An agent is a member of #billing like anyone else, under its owner's name. A mention routes to the owner's running session, which answers in the thread as Ana's agent, not as "Henosis": the voice belongs to the session. The answer is read-only by default; the context line says so, and the two buttons turn it into a directive or a team memory. The owner's stream gets one line, "Ana's agent answered Cy in #billing", so nothing happens behind the driver's back.

Trigger
app_mention of an agent's user, or the app with "@Ana's agent" in the text
Routes to

Shape

  • Placeholder first (Reading …), edited into the answer; never two messages.
  • Answers are at most ~120 words and one list; longer answers link to the session.
  • context: which session, what it read, the cost, and that the session did not change.
  • actions: Steer the session with this · Remember for the team · link.
Steersteer:payments:s_7f2:msg_ts Remembermemory.add:org_pay:msg_ts

Rules

  • A mention in a DM answers in the DM; in a channel the agent is not a member of, it answers ephemerally with "Add Ana's agent to #billing to let it answer here."
  • An answer costs the owner's budget; the context line shows it and the rail's token meter moves.
  • Mentions of a person ("@Bo") are Slack's own; the app only listens for agents.

The brief is the manager overview said in Slack: the sentence summary first, then what needs you, with the button that answers it, then the roster of sessions, then the numbers in Slack's two-column fields. It is computed from the ledger, never written by a model, so a lead can trust every number. When nothing needs anyone, the brief is one line and no buttons.

When · where

Schedule
8:00 in the project's timezone on working days; skipped when nothing ran and nothing waits
Channel
management channel; also to a lead's DM when they ask
Source
project.brief(), the same fleet brief the overview and the handoff use

blocks · in order

  • header · "Payments · Tuesday"
  • section · the sentence summary; "n need you" bold
  • divider
  • section · Needs you, each item with its link
  • section · Sessions, one line each: owner · title · status · step · tokens
  • section · fields · Tokens, Plans, Release gates, Joined
  • context · computed at · direction · the slash command
  • actions · Open the overview (primary) · Approvals · n · Contentions · n · link

Rules

  • Needs-you items are ordered as the inbox orders them: approvals, contentions, handoffs, then news.
  • Sessions are listed by owner, people first, paused last; a session older than a week says so.
  • Numbers are tabular and rounded to the nearest k; the budget bar is words, not a meter.
  • Six sessions or more fold into "and 4 more" with the link; the Slack message stays under 2,000 characters.
  • The brief never @mentions; the context line names the lead whose turn it is.
