Approval notices

The ordinary approval: an agent wants to run or reach something, the policy names who may say yes, and the notice asks them in one breath. The stars are there for a voice, not a vote; the same control, folded to the right. When it resolves, the card thins to a line, and a long stream folds the lines so the work stays readable.

1
The ask, in a sentence. Serif name, the verb, the argument in mono, the risk as a pill. "Ana's agent wants to run pnpm test (exec)". The apricot crease says this card is live; a release gate gets the navy→chocolate crease instead.
2
The rule and the person. "Needs one contributor or above" is the policy, verbatim from the kernel; "You can approve, Bo" is what it means for the reader; then where the agent is. Three facts, one line, dots between.
3
Two buttons, one hand. Approve is chocolate, Deny is quiet and only warms to danger on hover. The hint says who else was asked and that the first voice settles it, so Bo does not wait for Cy.
4
The voice, folded. Five small stars at the right, unlit, labelled "Rate it". A pick is optional: it rides along with the vote and shows in the divider ("Bo approved · 4 of 5"). Approve without a pick sends no number. Under a ratings rule the stars move left, grow, and gain the word; that is the rating-control exploration.
·
Keys. With the card under the cursor or focused: y approves, n denies, 1–5 picks a star first. Same keys in the queue and the strip.

·
Folding. Consecutive resolved approvals with nothing between them but steps collapse to one line: the count, the span, who answered, the classes. "Show" opens them in place. A denial never folds; it stays its own line in danger ink, because a denial is a decision someone will want to find.
1
The compact row. After the third approval in a session the pending card shrinks: one line, the ask on the left, the buttons on the right, the keys printed. The stars do not fit here; a press on 1–5 still rates, and the row grows to the full card while a star is picked.
2
The strip. The latest pending approval sits on the rail colour above the composer, so Bo can answer without finding the card. Approving in the strip answers the card; the strip crossfades to the next pending one or disappears. Two or more pending: "2 approvals wait" and g a opens the queue.
·
Rail and tab. While anything waits, the agent's card in the rail shows the amber dot and "waiting for Bo", and the tab title gets "(1)".
